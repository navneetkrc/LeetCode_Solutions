## Most Frequent Topics in Your LeetCode Problem List

### 1. Arrays & Strings
- **Most problems involve arrays directly or require string manipulations.**
- Examples from your list: Rotate Array, Max Subarray, Two Sum, Merge Intervals, Sliding Window, Product of Array Except Self, Longest Common Prefix, Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters.
- **Frequency**: Highest – arrays and strings are fundamental and show up in both easy and hard problems.[1][2][3]

### 2. Hash Table / Map
- Used to boost efficiency for lookups, frequency counting, and unique element tracking.
- Examples: Top K Frequent Elements, Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters, Two Sum, LRU/LFU Cache, Valid Sudoku, Word Ladder.
- **Frequency**: High – hash utilization is core for performance.[3][1]

### 3. Linked List
- Appears often, sometimes combined with stack/queue concepts.
- Examples: Reverse Linked List, Remove Nth Node, Merge K Sorted Lists, Flatten Nested List Iterator, LRU/LFU Cache.
- **Frequency**: High – linked lists cover multiple pointer and data handling techniques.[4][3]

### 4. Stack & Queue
- In problems involving order, reversals, or parenthesis matching.
- Examples: Valid Parentheses, Implement Queue Using Stacks, Stack-based elements in caches, Course Schedule (queue for topological sort).
- **Frequency**: Medium-High – essential for recursion and control flow applications.[1][4][3]

### 5. Tree & Graph (BFS, DFS, Topological Sort)
- Underpins many traversal/search and schedule problems.
- Examples: Number of Islands, Course Schedule I/II, Alien Dictionary, Word Ladder, Design Hit Counter, BFS variations (Rotting Oranges).
- **Frequency**: High – graphs/trees are behind most BFS/DFS-related questions.[4][1]

### 6. Sliding Window & Two Pointers
- Often used to optimize problems involving substrings or subarrays.
- Examples: Maximum Subarray, Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters, Subarray Product Less Than K.
- **Frequency**: High – unlocks many efficient solutions for what look like brute-force problems.[1][4]

### 7. Binary Search/Sorting
- Key for interval, matrix, or search optimization.
- Examples: Find Peak, Search a 2D Matrix II, Rotated Array, Merge Sorted Array, Median from Data Stream.
- **Frequency**: High for log(N) optimizations and sorted data.[3][1]

### 8. Dynamic Programming
- Required for stock trading, substrings, optimal coin/buy-sell scheduling.
- Examples: Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock (multiple variants), Word Break, Course Schedule (with memoization).
- **Frequency**: Medium – shows up in optimization-type or state-tracking problems.[4][3][1]

### 9. Design & OOD
- LRU/LFU Cache, Snapshot Array, Randomized Set/Hit Counter, Time-based Key Value Store.
- Demands knowledge of system design, not just basic data structures.
- **Frequency**: Medium – but increasing, especially for senior interview levels.[3][1]

## Takeaway

If you are preparing for interviews with this company, **focus first on mastering array/strings, hash maps, two pointers/sliding window, BFS/DFS on graphs and trees, and core data structures like stacks, queues, and linked lists**. Design/OOD and DP are important for advanced roles, but the most "bang for buck" is in the classic algorithmic patterns that appear repeatedly.

Focusing on mastering these topics will maximize your coverage of the most frequent and critical LeetCode problem types for company interviews.[4][3][1]
